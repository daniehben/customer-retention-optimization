# Phase 5.A — Retention Policy Framework (Conservative + Exploratory)

## Purpose of the Policy Layer

The purpose of Phase 5 is to translate the offer-economics engine into an explicit **retention policy** that is safe for a financially constrained business.

Phase 5 does not try to “force incentives to work.” It accepts Phase 4’s economics as the constraint and defines **when not to intervene**, and when intervention can be considered without harming the business.

This policy exists to prevent value-destructive actions and ensure every incentive decision is auditable.

---

## Inputs from Phase 4 (What we accept as constraints)

Phase 4 established the following realities:

* **The EV engine is extremely sensitive to baseline validity.** When segmentation leaks future outcomes, baseline probabilities collapse and EV collapses into “cost-only” logic.
* After removing leakage and rebuilding baselines using **decision-time feature segments** (recency + AOV tiers), the system behaves realistically:

  * **Most incentives remain negative EV**
  * **No-offer is the dominant action for the majority**
  * A **small subset** can justify targeted intervention (most often free shipping), depending on value and cost conditions

These findings mean incentives are not a default retention tool — they are a controlled option that must pass policy filters.

---

## Default Retention Stance: No Intervention

The default action of the system is **no_offer**.

Customers are assumed to follow their natural reorder behavior unless there is strong evidence that:

1. intervention is economically defensible, and
2. intervention is unlikely to create margin leakage.

Non-intervention is not “doing nothing.” It is an intentional decision to preserve cash and avoid subsidizing baseline behavior.

---

## Role of Monetary Incentives (Last-resort, not routine)

Monetary incentives are treated as **exceptional actions**, not a standard retention mechanism.

Even when EV can be positive for a small subset, incentives still carry structural downside risk:

* they can subsidize customers who would buy anyway
* they reduce margin immediately (cash-real)
* they can create dependency if used broadly

Therefore, Phase 5 only allows incentives through strict controls:

* **Eligibility gates** (Phase 5.B) to prevent wasteful consideration
* **EV re-evaluation on eligible subset** (Phase 5.C)
* **Best-offer ranking with no-offer fallback** (Phase 5.D)

This ensures the system is conservative by design, but still exploratory within safe bounds.

---

## Role of Machine Learning in Phase 5 (Refinement, not expansion)

Machine learning is not used to justify broad incentive usage.

Its role is **risk refinement inside already policy-eligible space**, helping differentiate customers within high-risk / high-stakes cases.

ML is a prioritization tool, not a decision maker:

* ML does not override unit economics
* ML does not independently trigger incentives
* ML only helps rank or identify extreme-risk cases once a customer is already within the allowed policy boundary

---

## What This Phase Does Not Do

Phase 5.A does not:

* assign offers
* define numeric thresholds (that happens in 5.B gates)
* implement SQL logic
* re-estimate lift or cost assumptions
* optimize budgets

It defines the **decision philosophy** and safety constraints that the technical layers implement.

---

## Transition to Phase 5.B

With the policy stance defined (“no offer by default; incentives only under strict controls”), Phase 5.B implements the first enforcement layer:

> **Eligibility gates** that filter the population to a small, defensible subset before EV ranking or ML refinement is applied.

---


## Key Lessons / Why this matters 

This policy layer is what turns the project from “I computed EV” into a **real decision system**. In many retention projects, teams jump straight to picking offers and end up subsidizing customers who would have purchased anyway—quietly destroying margin. Here, the model is designed to **protect the business first**: *no offer* is the default, incentives are treated as risky tools, and customers must pass explicit gates before any offer is even considered. Machine learning (when added) is used only to refine prioritization inside that safe boundary—not to justify broader discounting. The result is a retention approach that is **deployable, auditable, and financially responsible**, which is exactly what low-margin businesses need.
