# Phase 5.A — Retention Policy Framework

## Purpose of the Policy Layer

The purpose of Phase 5 is to translate the economic findings of Phase 4 into an explicit, conservative retention policy.
This policy defines how retention actions should be approached in a financially constrained environment where monetary incentives carry meaningful downside risk.

Phase 5 does not attempt to reverse or optimize the economics discovered in Phase 4. Instead, it accepts those findings as ground truth and focuses on protecting the business from value-destructive actions.

## Inputs from Phase 4
Phase 5 is grounded in the following conclusions from Phase 4 (Offer Economics):

* All tested monetary incentives (discounts and free shipping) produce negative expected value under conservative assumptions.

* Incremental profit from incentives is negligible, while expected costs remain strictly positive.

* Non-intervention dominates intervention for the majority of customers.

* Monetary incentives primarily discount baseline behavior rather than creating new demand.

These findings establish that monetary incentives cannot be treated as standard retention tools under current business conditions.

## Default Retention Stance

The default action of the retention system is no intervention.

Customers are assumed to follow their natural reorder behavior unless there is strong evidence that intervention is both necessary and economically defensible.

Non-intervention is not treated as inaction or neglect. It is an intentional decision aimed at preserving margin, avoiding unnecessary cost, and preventing value leakage.

Any deviation from this default stance must be explicitly justified by subsequent policy layers.

## Role of Monetary Incentives

Monetary incentives are treated as exceptional, last-resort actions, not as routine retention mechanisms.

Given their negative expected value in Phase 4, incentives are not deployed broadly or automatically. Instead, they are conceptually reserved for extreme cases where:

* The risk of losing the customer is unusually high, and

* The potential cost of churn is greater than the expected cost of intervention.

Even in such cases, incentives are not guaranteed actions; they are candidates for further scrutiny.

This policy reflects the principle that **restraint is often more valuable than action in low-margin environments**.

## Role of Machine Learning in Phase 5

Machine learning is not used to justify or expand incentive usage.

Its role in Phase 5 is limited to risk refinement, helping to differentiate customers within already high-risk segments. ML serves as a secondary input that supports more precise decision-making, particularly in identifying extreme-risk cases.

Machine learning outputs do not override economic logic and do not independently trigger retention actions.

## What This Phase Does Not Do

Phase 5.A does not:

* Assign incentives to customers

* Define eligibility thresholds

* Specify intervention rules

* Implement SQL logic

* Optimize or re-estimate lift or cost assumptions

These tasks are intentionally deferred to later phases to maintain conceptual clarity and auditability.

## Transition to Phase 5.B

With the retention philosophy and default stance defined, the next phase focuses on economic eligibility rules.
Phase 5.B will determine which customers, if any, are eligible for further consideration based on risk and value constraints—before any machine learning refinement is applied.
