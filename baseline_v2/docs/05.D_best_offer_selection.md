## Phase 5.D — Best Offer per Customer (Decision Ranking Engine)

## Purpose

Phase 5.D turns the Phase 5.C simulation grid into a single decision per customer anchor:

> Choose the offer with the highest net expected value, but only intervene if the best EV is positive.

This is where the system becomes decision-shaped.

## View produced

`churn.v_6_phase5d_best_offer_per_customer`

One row per:

* `(customer_unique_id, anchor_date)`

With:

* `max_ev_over_anchor`
* `best_offer_type` (with `no_offer` fallback)

## Ranking logic (what you implemented)

Offers within each customer anchor were ranked as:

1. Primary: `net_ev_default` descending
2. Tie-break: cheaper offer (`offer_cost`) ascending
3. Final tie-break: deterministic ordering by `offer_type`

Then a hard business rule was applied:

* If `max_ev_over_anchor <= 0` → force `best_offer_type = 'no_offer'`

This prevents the system from “choosing the least bad incentive.”
It will only spend money when it expects to create value.

## What the final results mean (business interpretation)

The output shows:

* **734 → `no_offer`**
* **42 → `free_shipping`**

This is a shape that makes sense for a small, margin-sensitive business:

* Most customers do not justify incentive spend.
* A small subset does — and the winning incentive is the one with **bounded cost** (free shipping), not revenue-scaled discounts.

## Why free shipping wins only sometimes (the important nuance)

Earlier analysis already hinted at this:

* free shipping has a **very negative min EV**
* but it also has **some high positive cases**

The business story:

* shipping subsidies are dangerous when the freight burden is high (you lose money fast)
* but for a small subset where freight is manageable *and* the uplift assumption moves probability enough, free shipping can become economically justified

So the policy doesn’t say “free shipping is good.”
It says: **free shipping is only good when the customer’s economics make it good.**

## What changed vs the “collapsed” version (why this is now credible)

* When risk bands were built from **days_to_next_order**, baseline probability estimates became near-deterministic.
* That made `delta_p_default ≈ 0` for most rows.
* When Δp collapses, EV collapses:

  * incremental value ~ 0
  * only incentive cost remains → negative EV everywhere
  * decision engine degenerates into “always no_offer” or “random artifacts.”

After rebuilding segmentation using **recency bands + AOV tiers**, you removed leakage, restored probabilistic variation, and made EV comparisons meaningful again — producing a stable, realistic policy.
