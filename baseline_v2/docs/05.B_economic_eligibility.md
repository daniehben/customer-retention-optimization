# Phase 5.B — Intervention Eligibility Rules (Policy Layer)

## Purpose of Phase 5.B

Phase 5.B defines **who is allowed to enter the incentive decision system**.

The goal is **not** to maximize retention at any cost, but to:

* prevent incentives from being evaluated for structurally unprofitable customers
* reserve incentives for customers where intervention has a defensible chance of being net-positive
* keep the system **fully explainable and auditable**

This phase is intentionally **rule-based (not ML)** so the business keeps control over *why* someone is eligible.

---

## High-Level Design

Eligibility is determined through a **three-gate policy framework**:

1. **Risk Gate** – Is the customer meaningfully at churn risk *based on decision-time features*?
2. **Value Gate** – Has the customer historically generated sufficient value (AOV proxy)?
3. **Structural Cost Gate** – Is the customer structurally too expensive to retain (freight burden)?

Only customers who pass **all three gates** are marked `eligible_for_review`.

---

## Why Gate 1 is Feature-Based (and not outcome-based)

Earlier, “risk bands” were defined using `days_to_next_order`. That creates **future leakage** in a decision system, because at decision time you wouldn’t know the customer’s future next-order timing.

Phase 5.B therefore relies on **feature-based risk segments** built from **pre-anchor behavior**, specifically:

* `recency_band` (how long since last order at the anchor)
* `aov_tier` (value proxy based on historical AOV)
* combined into `risk_segment = recency_band || ' | ' || aov_tier`

This change is what prevents the EV engine from collapsing (because baselines remain probabilistic rather than becoming “0/1 by definition”).

---

## Gate 1 — Risk Segment Eligibility

**Objective:**
Only consider incentives for customers who are plausibly at risk *at decision time*.

**Rule (policy intent):**
Only customers in the most at-risk recency groups proceed (typically the equivalent of **Cold/Dormant** recency behavior).

**Rationale:**
Intervening on Active/Warm customers increases the chance of subsidizing baseline purchases (margin leakage) without creating incremental behavior.

**Ineligibility label:**
`risk_band_not_eligible` *(kept as the label name for continuity, even though the gate is now segment-based)*

---

## Gate 2 — Value Eligibility (AOV Threshold)

**Objective:**
Avoid spending incentives on customers with weak value signals.

### Initial approach (p25) and why it changed

AOV is used as a proxy for value. The first threshold tested was **p25 of AOV**, computed **within the Gate-1 eligible population** (so thresholds reflect the relevant at-risk group).

Results showed p25 was too permissive:

* it admitted too many low-value customers
* eligibility became overly broad
* it weakened the “conservative but exploratory” stance

### Final policy threshold (p35)

The threshold was intentionally raised to:

> **p35 AOV within each Gate-1 eligible segment (or band-equivalent group)**

This tightened eligibility while remaining slightly permissive (still exploratory), and reduced the chance of spending incentives on marginal customers.

This is a **policy choice**, not statistical “optimization.”

### Value gate outcomes (two distinct cases)

1. **Missing AOV**

* AOV is `NULL`
* excluded because the model cannot defend spending without a value signal
  **Label:** `missing_aov`

2. **Below value threshold**

* AOV exists, but `aov_pre_anchor < p35 threshold`
  **Label:** `below_value_threshold`

Splitting these cases improves auditability and storytelling: “unknown” is different from “known low.”

---

## Gate 3 — Structural Cost Exclusion (Freight Burden)

**Objective:**
Exclude customers whose cost structure makes incentives economically irrational.

**Rule:** customers are excluded if either:

* `avg_freight_pre_anchor` is missing (conservative stance), or
* `avg_freight_pre_anchor > 0.5 * aov_pre_anchor`

**Rationale:**
When freight is too high relative to order value, even retained orders struggle to generate enough contribution margin to offset incentive cost.

**Ineligibility labels:**

* `missing_freight`
* `high_freight_cost`

---

## Final Eligibility Decision

A customer is marked:

* `eligible_for_review` if:

  * Gate 1 passed
  * Gate 2 passed
  * Gate 3 passed (i.e., not structurally excluded)

* `ineligible` otherwise, with a **single dominant reason** recorded.

This design guarantees:

* deterministic outcomes
* clean downstream analysis
* clear human-readable explanations

---

## Final Ineligibility Breakdown

The eligibility output includes mutually exclusive ineligibility reasons:

* `risk_band_not_eligible`
* `missing_aov`
* `below_value_threshold`
* `missing_freight`
* `high_freight_cost`

These labels do not change decisions — they exist to explain them.

---

## Key Takeaways from Phase 5.B

* Eligibility is **intentionally constrained**, not optimized for volume
* Thresholds are policy-driven and adjustable
* Missing data is treated differently from low value
* Structural cost is treated as a hard exclusion
* The system is transparent, explainable, and business-safe

Phase 5.B creates a controlled, defensible candidate pool so Phase 5.C can focus only on **offer-level EV comparison** for customers who are actually worth reviewing.

---

## Transition to Phase 5.C

With eligibility defined, the next question becomes:

> “For the eligible customers, what does EV look like across each offer — and does *any* offer beat no_offer?”

Phase 5.C constructs a **customer × offer EV spine** for eligible customers only, so that Phase 5.D can rank and select the best action with a no-offer fallback.
