# Phase 5.B — Intervention Eligibility Rules (Policy Layer)

## Purpose of Phase 5.B

Phase 5.B defines **which customers are even allowed to enter the incentive decision system**.

The goal is **not** to maximize retention at any cost, but to:

* prevent incentives from being wasted on structurally unprofitable customers
* ensure incentives are reserved for customers where intervention has a reasonable chance of positive economic return
* keep the system **fully explainable and auditable**

This phase intentionally uses **rule-based logic** rather than machine learning to ensure transparency and business control.

---

## High-Level Design

Eligibility is determined through a **three-gate policy framework**:

1. **Risk Gate** – Is the customer at meaningful churn risk?
2. **Value Gate** – Has the customer historically generated sufficient value?
3. **Structural Cost Gate** – Is the customer structurally too expensive to retain?

Only customers who pass **all three gates** are marked as `eligible_for_review`.

---

## Gate 1 — Risk Band Eligibility

**Objective:**
Ensure incentives are only considered for customers who are genuinely at risk of churn.

**Rule:**
Only customers in the following risk bands are eligible to proceed:

* `High risk (121–180d)`
* `Dormant / very high risk (180+d or none)`

All other customers are automatically excluded.

**Rationale:**
Low- and medium-risk customers are still active and do not require incentives.
Intervening too early increases cost without improving retention outcomes.

**Ineligibility label:**
`risk_band_not_eligible`

---

## Gate 2 — Value Eligibility (AOV-Based Threshold)

**Objective:**
Ensure incentives are not spent on customers with insufficient historical value.

### Initial Approach

The value gate uses **Average Order Value (AOV)** as a proxy for customer value.

Initially, the **25th percentile (p25)** of AOV within each eligible risk band was tested as the threshold.

However, exploratory analysis showed that:

* p25 admitted a very large share of low-spend customers
* eligibility rates exceeded 60–70% in some high-risk segments
* the gate was not sufficiently selective

### Threshold Refinement

After analyzing the AOV distribution within eligible risk bands, the threshold was **intentionally raised** to:

> **35th percentile (p35) of AOV per risk band**

This change:

* tightened eligibility without eliminating large portions of the population
* preserved a *slightly permissive but still conservative* policy
* reduced the risk of incentives being applied to marginal customers

This refinement reflects a deliberate **policy tradeoff**, not a statistical optimization.

---

### Value Gate Outcomes

The value gate now explicitly distinguishes between two fundamentally different cases:

#### 1. Missing Behavioral Data

Customers with **NULL AOV** are excluded due to insufficient historical information.

**Label:**
`missing_aov`

**Interpretation:**
The system cannot confidently assess value, so incentives are deferred rather than risked.

---

#### 2. Below Value Threshold

Customers with AOV **below the p35 threshold** for their risk band.

**Label:**
`below_value_threshold`

**Interpretation:**
These customers have observable behavior but historically low spend, making incentive ROI unlikely.

---

This decomposition improves:

* auditability
* storytelling
* future policy extensibility

---

## Gate 3 — Structural Cost Exclusion (Freight Burden)

**Objective:**
Exclude customers whose cost structure makes incentives economically irrational.

**Rule:**
Customers are excluded if:

```
avg_freight_pre_anchor > 50% of aov_pre_anchor
```

or if freight data is missing.

**Rationale:**
For these customers, even successful retention is unlikely to recover incentive and shipping costs.

**Ineligibility label:**
`high_freight_cost`

---

## Final Eligibility Decision

A customer is marked as:

* `eligible_for_review` if:

  * risk gate passed
  * value gate passed
  * structural cost gate passed

* `ineligible` otherwise, with a **single dominant exclusion reason** recorded for transparency.

This ensures:

* deterministic outcomes
* no double-counting of failure reasons
* clean downstream analysis

---

## Final Ineligibility Breakdown (Interpretation Layer)

The final eligibility view produces the following mutually exclusive ineligibility reasons:

* `below_value_threshold`
* `missing_aov`
* `risk_band_not_eligible`
* `high_freight_cost`

These labels do **not** change decisions — they exist to explain them.

---

## Key Takeaways from Phase 5.B

* Eligibility is **intentionally constrained**, not optimized for volume
* Thresholds are policy-driven and adjustable
* Missing data is treated differently from low value
* Structural cost is treated as a hard exclusion
* The system is transparent, explainable, and business-safe

Phase 5.B establishes a **controlled candidate pool**, enabling Phase 5.C to focus exclusively on **offer-level expected value optimization**.

---

## Transition to Phase 5.C

With eligibility defined, the next step is to answer:

> *Given eligible customers, which incentive (if any) should be applied — and why?*

Phase 5.C will:

* evaluate expected value by offer
* rank interventions within the eligible pool
* introduce budget-aware decision logic

No further eligibility changes are expected.
